<h1><strong>Are You Destined to Meet Your Soulmate?&nbsp;</strong></h1>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">No research I found shows that everyone has one destined soulmate waiting to be met. Psych Central puts it plainly: science does not set out to prove soulmates exist.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">What the research does show is that many people believe in them (60% of US adults in a 2021 YouGov poll) and that the way you think about a soulmate can shape how a relationship goes.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">People who see love as something that grows with effort tend to hold up better over time than people who expect a perfect, effortless match.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This guide covers what a soulmate is, whether soulmates are real, whether everyone finds or meets theirs, the signs people describe, how to find your soulmate (or why you may not have yet), what to do when you miss someone you thought was the one, and how to judge services that promise to reveal yours.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It is general information, not relationship or mental health advice.</span></p>

<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The honest answer is that nobody can say, because "soulmate" is a belief, not a measurable thing.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Some people feel they met theirs, some feel they haven't yet and many feel the idea doesn't fit them at all.&nbsp;</span></p>

<h2><strong>Who Is a Soulmate? What Is the Definition of a Soulmate?</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">What is the definition of a soulmate? Psych Central's article</span><a href="https://www.psychcentral.com/relationships/soul-mates-do-they-really-exist"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">Soul Mates: Do They Really Exist?</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, by Jenna Fletcher and medically reviewed by Lori Lawrenz, PsyD (updated December 11, 2023), describes soulmates as "separate souls that share a deep romantic or platonic connection."&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">So who is a soulmate? It depends on the person.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For some it means a romantic partner who completes them, and for others a friend, mentor or someone who changes their life.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Not every definition is romantic, and that is part of why the question has no single answer.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For soulmate meaning in love and what does soulmate mean in a relationship, the idea often carries two assumptions: that the match is destined, and that it will feel effortless.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Research in the next sections suggests those assumptions are the risky part.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Healthline's piece on twin flames (a related idea) says there is no scientific evidence that they exist either, though some psychologists find the idea worth exploring.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Are Soulmates Real? Do Soulmates Exist?</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Are soulmates real? Do soulmates exist? Are soul mates real? Are soulmates real? Science has no test for it, and Psych Central says the notion has no scientific support, so I can't call them real or unreal.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">People still believe. YouGov's</span><a href="https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/34094-soulmates-poll-survey-data"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">soulmates poll</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, published February 10, 2021 (14,994 US adults surveyed January 27 to February 3, 2021), found 60% believe in soulmates, 23% don't and 18% are unsure.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Women (64%) were more likely than men (55%) to believe.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Belief was higher among those in civil partnerships (66%) and marriages (59%) than among single people (49%).</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">These are 2021 figures, and other polls have given different numbers: a Monmouth University poll (January 2017, 801 adults) found about two-thirds believed.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I did not find a newer national poll, and the numbers vary by how the question is worded.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Does everyone have a soulmate? Believers don't agree either.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A YouGov poll from April 2015, which I saw only in a search excerpt, found 25% of Americans believed each person has only one soulmate and 33% believed we can have more than one.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">So even among believers, "the one" is not universal.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Destiny Beliefs Versus Growth Beliefs</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This is where the research gets practical. Relationship scientists describe two mindsets: destiny beliefs (a relationship is either meant to be or it isn't) and growth beliefs (a relationship improves through effort).&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The Monmouth University Polling Institute's</span><a href="https://www.monmouth.edu/polling-institute/reports/MonmouthPoll_US_020917"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">2017 national poll report</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> (January 12 to 15, 2017; 801 adults; margin of error 3.5 points) quotes psychology professor Gary Lewandowski saying that "those who believe in soulmates and destiny, are actually more likely to break up," while those who believe relationships grow over time "have more stable relationships."&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That is one researcher's summary in a poll release, not a study.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Other work points the same way. Psych Central describes a 2004 study by Franiuk and colleagues finding that people with a soulmate theory were more satisfied only when they felt they had found "the one," even under stress.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A 2024 study in the </span><em><span style="font-weight: 400;">European Journal of Personality</span></em><span style="font-weight: 400;">, which I read about only through an Upworthy report by Tod Perry (July 8, 2024), followed 904 couples in Germany, Switzerland and Austria for two years.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It reported that destiny believers were happier at the start, but satisfaction fell less sharply for people with stronger growth beliefs.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I did not read the original paper, so treat that as a secondary report.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The takeaway is modest: believing in a soulmate is not harmful in itself, but expecting a relationship to work without effort seems to be.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Signs You Found Your Soulmate: Soulmate Signs</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">People search for </span><a href="https://modest-cuckoo-12znf5f.mystrikingly.com/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">signs you found your soulmate</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, soulmate signs, signs of soulmate, soulmate connection signs and physical signs you met your soulmate.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Psych Central says there is no scientifically validated way to identify one.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The signs it lists are anecdotal: feeling you "just know" early on, a strong gut feeling, agreeing on big and small things, sharing a sense of humor and feeling at peace with the person.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">So how to know when you found your soulmate, and how do you know if you've found your soulmate? You can't confirm it with a test.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A soulmate connection or the soulmate connections people describe usually include comfort, trust, shared values and a sense of being understood, which are also features of a healthy relationship in general.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I found no evidence for physical signs (such as a particular sensation or sign on the body) that reveal a soulmate, and I would be skeptical of any list that claims otherwise.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For how to identify soulmate questions or how do I know I found my soulmate, a safer question is: do I feel respected, safe and able to be myself, and do we handle problems well together?</span></p>
<h2><strong>How to Find Your Soulmate: Where Is My Soulmate?</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">If you ask how to find your soulmate, how to find my soulmate, how do I find my soulmate, how do you find your soulmate, how to find soulmate or how to find my soul mate, the sourced answer is less mystical than a map.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Psych Central mentions a 2021 study that found four precursors to romantic love: reciprocal liking, familiarity, social influence and having emotional needs met.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It also notes commonly suggested preparation, such as knowing yourself, being comfortable alone and practicing self-acceptance.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That points to something practical: connection tends to grow from meeting people, spending time together and being liked back.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">From general knowledge, not checked against a live source here, widening your social circle, joining groups built around your interests and being honest about what you want help you meet more people.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">When people ask where is my soulmate or where's my soulmate, there is no location or date to point to.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I found no source that predicts when or where anyone will meet a partner.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Will I Ever Meet My Soulmate? Why Can't I Find My Soulmate?</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Will I ever meet my soulmate? Will I ever find my soulmate? Nobody can promise it, and be wary of anyone who does.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Why can't I find my soulmate? There are many possible reasons, and I can't tell which applies to you.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">From general knowledge, flagged as not sourced here, an unrealistic checklist, waiting for a perfect feeling, limited chances to meet people or wanting something different from a long-term partner can all play a role.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A psychologist quoted by Newsweek (Alexandra Solomon, Northwestern University's Family Institute), via a reposted article, says people who define a soulmate as a perfect match tend to report more conflict, while those who see a soulmate as someone to grow with tend to have sturdier relationships.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I read this only in a repost, so treat it as a lead. Many people also lead full lives without a long-term partner.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Missing Your Soulmate</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Missing your soulmate can mean grieving a breakup, a loss or a relationship that never happened.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It is a normal and painful experience, and it doesn't prove the person was your only match.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Reaching out to friends, giving yourself time and talking with a counselor can help.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">If sadness or longing is affecting your sleep, work or daily life, or you have thoughts of harming yourself, contact a doctor, a mental health professional or a local crisis line.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This guidance is general, not drawn from the soulmate research above.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Meeting a Soulmate: A Practical Way to Think About It</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Meeting a soulmate, if you believe in the idea, is not the finish line.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">If you keep one idea from the research, keep this: treat a strong connection as a starting point, and keep listening, compromising and working on the relationship.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That fits the growth beliefs the studies favor and doesn't ask you to give up on romance.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Frequently Asked Questions</strong></h2>
<h3><strong>Is it true that you meet your soulmate?</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">There is no scientific evidence that everyone has or meets a destined soulmate. Many people believe in the idea, and some feel they found theirs.</span></p>
<h3><strong>Are soulmates real?</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Science doesn't test them. Psych Central says the notion has no scientific support, but belief is common: 60% of US adults in a 2021 YouGov poll.</span></p>
<h3><strong>How do I know if I've found my soulmate?</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">No validated test exists. People describe feeling at ease, aligned on values and deeply understood. Whether the relationship is healthy matters more than whether the label fits.</span></p>
<h3><strong>Does everyone have a soulmate?</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Unknown. Even believers disagree: in a 2015 YouGov poll, 25% thought each person has one and 33% thought we can have more than one.</span></p>
<h3><strong>How do I find my soulmate?</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Research points to meeting people, building familiarity and mutual liking, and knowing yourself. No method guarantees a match.</span></p>
<h3><strong>Is believing in soulmates bad for relationships?</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Not by itself, but research suggests destiny beliefs can be riskier than growth beliefs when problems arise.</span></p>
